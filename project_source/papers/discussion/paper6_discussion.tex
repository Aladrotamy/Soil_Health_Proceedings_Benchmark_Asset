\section{Discussion}

The combined analysis of the nitrogen trial shows why multi-site trials are
analysed in two stages. The significant interaction between site and nitrogen
means that a single mean response would misrepresent every site, and the
results are therefore reported site by site in Paper~2. The same applies to
the tillage trial of Paper~4, in which both the size and the direction of the
yield response differed between sites. Testing the interaction first, and
choosing the level of reporting from its outcome, is the step most often
omitted when results from several sites are summarised \parencite{gomez1984}.

Three sites are enough to show that treatment effects vary with environment,
but not to explain why. Site differs from site in soil, climate and history at
once, and with one site per climatic zone those factors cannot be separated.
Explaining an interaction requires more sites, or sites chosen so that one
factor varies while the others are held constant, as large syntheses across
many trials have done \parencite{pittelkow2015}.

Soil health research adds the further problem of combining several responses.
Converting indicators to scores, as in Paper~1, puts them on a common scale,
but the choices of scoring limits and weights shape the result and must be
reported with it \parencite{andrews2004,bunemann2018}. The conventions
described here, a common design at every site, one register of plots, analysis
of each site before combining, and explicit reporting rules, are intended to
make those choices visible and the results reproducible.

\section{Conclusion}

All trials in this volume share a randomised complete block design with four
blocks at three sites, a single plot register and a common sequence of
analysis. In the nitrogen trial, the site by nitrogen interaction was
significant (\res{p6-P-sitexnitrogen}), and two site-by-rate means had to
differ by more than \res{p6-lsd}~\si{\tonne\per\hectare} to be distinguished.
Multi-site results should be reported by site whenever such an interaction is
present. The example values are synthetic.
