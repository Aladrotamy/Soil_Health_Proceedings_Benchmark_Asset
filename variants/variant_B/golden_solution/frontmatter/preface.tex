\chapter{Editor's Preface}

Soil is the foundation of crop production, yet its condition changes slowly
and is easily overlooked until yields begin to fall. The idea of soil health
treats the soil as a living system whose chemical, physical and biological
properties together decide how well it supports crops, stores water and
carbon, and recovers from disturbance. Measuring that condition, and managing
land so that it improves rather than declines, is a shared concern of
agronomists, soil scientists and farmers in every climatic zone.

This volume brings together six contributed papers presented at the
International Symposium on Soil Health and Sustainable Crop Production. They
were chosen to span the main questions of the field: how soil health is
indicated and measured, how nutrients and organic matter are managed, how
crops respond to different systems of tillage, intercropping and rotation, and
how results from field trials are analysed and reported. Each paper is
self-contained, with its own abstract and list of references, and each can be
read and typeset on its own.

All trial data in this volume are synthetic, as the imprint explains. They
were designed to behave like realistic field measurements and to obey the
rules set out in the methods of each paper, so that the volume can serve as a
faithful example of how such work is written, checked and published.
