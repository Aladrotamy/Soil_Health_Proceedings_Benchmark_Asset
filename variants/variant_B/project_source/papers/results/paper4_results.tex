\section{Results}

\subsection{Soil organic carbon}
Over five years, topsoil organic carbon declined under conventional tillage
with residues removed and increased under every other combination
(Table~\ref{tab:p4means}, Figure~\ref{fig:p4dsoc}). The largest gains were
under no-till with residues retained: \res{p4-dsoc-S1-NTR1},
\res{p4-dsoc-S2-NTR1} and \res{p4-dsoc-S3-NTR1}~\si{\gram\per\kilo\gram} at S1,
S2 and S3, against \res{p4-dsoc-S1-CTR0}, \res{p4-dsoc-S2-CTR0} and
\res{p4-dsoc-S3-CTR0}~\si{\gram\per\kilo\gram} under conventional tillage with
residues removed. Both main effects were significant at every site
(Table~\ref{tab:p4anova}). The interaction was significant at S1
($F=\res{p4-dsoc-interaction-F-S1}$, \res{p4-dsoc-interaction-P-S1}) and S3
($F=\res{p4-dsoc-interaction-F-S3}$, \res{p4-dsoc-interaction-P-S3}), where
no-till gained more carbon when residues were retained, but not at the
semi-arid site S2 ($F=\res{p4-dsoc-interaction-F-S2}$,
\res{p4-dsoc-interaction-P-S2}).

\subsection{Grain yield}
Yield responded differently from carbon. At the temperate site S1, no-till
reduced yield ($F=\res{p4-yield-tillage-F-S1}$, \res{p4-yield-tillage-P-S1}),
most clearly when residues were removed (\res{p4-yield-S1-NTR0} against
\res{p4-yield-S1-CTR0}~\si{\tonne\per\hectare}). At the semi-arid site S2 the
effect of tillage depended on residues (interaction
$F=\res{p4-yield-interaction-F-S2}$, \res{p4-yield-interaction-P-S2}): no-till
with residues retained gave the highest yield of the four combinations,
\res{p4-yield-S2-NTR1}~\si{\tonne\per\hectare}, and no-till with residues
removed the lowest, \res{p4-yield-S2-NTR0}~\si{\tonne\per\hectare}. At the humid
tropical site S3 neither factor affected yield significantly
(tillage \res{p4-yield-tillage-P-S3}; residues \res{p4-yield-residue-P-S3}).
